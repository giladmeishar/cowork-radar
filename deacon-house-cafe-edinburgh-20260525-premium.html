By day

A councilor, a cabinet-maker.

Deacon William Brodie was a fixture of polite Edinburgh society — respected craftsman, councilor of the Incorporation of Wrights & Masons, regular at the Cape Club. He fitted the locks of half the houses on the Royal Mile.

The cafe inherits the daylight half of his life: open early, no booking, sit and eat, leave a tip if you liked it.

By night

A burglar, a libertine.

While the city slept he returned to the locks he'd fitted — using wax impressions of the keys, a gang of three accomplices, and a passion for cock-fighting that ran his finances into the ground.

He was hanged on the 1st of October, 1788, on the gallows he himself had improved. Robert Louis Stevenson used him as the seed for Jekyll & Hyde.

No. 03

The close, today.
Mostly unchanged.

The arch is the original 17th-century one. The flagstones are the original ones. The cafe is open — go in, sit down, ask for a scone and a pot of tea. The kitchen closes mid-afternoon, before the city wakes up.
